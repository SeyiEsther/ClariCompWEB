01 · Distance calculation

Haversine for aviation. Distance Matrix for ground.

Flights are calculated using the Haversine Great Circle formula. This is the geodesic distance between two points on a sphere - the path an aircraft actually follows, give or take routing.

d = 2r · asin(√(sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁ · cos φ₂ · sin²(Δλ/2)))

where
  r   = earth radius (6,371 km)
  φ₁,φ₂ = latitudes of origin and destination
  Δφ  = φ₂ - φ₁
  Δλ  = λ₂ - λ₁

Road, rail and taxi journeys use the Google Maps Distance Matrix API to return real-route distance, accounting for actual road or track geometry. Where origin/destination is ambiguous, ClariComp falls back to the centroid of the named locality and flags the row.

02 · Emission factors

DEFRA 2025 GHG Conversion Factor tables, Table 5.

All factors are sourced from the UK Government's 2025 release of the GHG conversion-factor tables, specifically Table 5 - Business Travel. Each row in your ClariComp file is annotated with the factor reference used, so an auditor can trace any calculation back to a published source.

Factor updates are versioned. The 2025 release applies to reporting periods ending in 2025. Earlier periods use the corresponding earlier release - ClariComp does not retroactively apply newer factors to older data.

03 · Factor precision

Six decimal places. Carried through every step.

Rounding errors accumulate. ClariComp stores every factor at 6 dp, carries that precision through distance × factor, and only rounds at the final aggregate. The published report shows three decimal places of tonnes CO₂e; the audit appendix shows six.

04 · Confidence scoring

A score per row, aggregated per report.

Each row carries a confidence score from 0 to 100. The score is reduced when (a) the transport class is inferred rather than specified, (b) origin or destination is resolved to a centroid, (c) the trip is round-trip but only logged one-way, or (d) distance is not provided and Distance Matrix returns multiple plausible routes.

The aggregate confidence is the row-emission-weighted mean. A 98.7% confidence on a 47 tCO₂e total means the methodology applied is unambiguous for 98.7% of the emissions accounted for.

05 · Assumptions

What ClariComp assumes when your data is incomplete.

  1. Aircraft class defaults to economy if not specified. Business and first carry materially different factors; ClariComp flags rows where class is inferred.
  2. Trips logged with a single date but a return implied (e.g. day-trip Manchester ↔ London) are treated as one-way unless a return row is present.
  3. Car journeys default to "average passenger car, unknown fuel" if engine size and fuel type are absent.
  4. Rail journeys outside the UK national rail network use the European rail factor if the route is fully European, the long-haul rail factor otherwise.
  5. Where two airports serve the same city (London, Paris, Milan), ClariComp uses the airport name if provided, else the city centroid.
06 · Limitations

What ClariComp does not do.

ClariComp covers Scope 3 Category 6 - Business Travel - only. It does not calculate Scope 1 (direct fuel), Scope 2 (electricity), or other Scope 3 categories (purchased goods, transport of sold products, employee commuting, etc.). The preview output is indicative, not an audited statement.

If you need full-scope reporting or third-party assurance, ClariComp is not the right tool. The product is deliberately narrow - done properly, at one price - and we'd rather tell you that here than at the end of a sales call.

07 · Standards alignment

GHG Protocol Corporate Standard · Scope 3 Category 6.

ClariComp follows the GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting & Reporting Standard, with calculations specifically aligned to the Scope 3 Standard's Category 6 (Business Travel) - the category covering employee travel between sites and to client engagements in third-party transport.

08 · What this means for your audit

The formula trail, in practice.

Every row in your ClariComp file carries: the input fields (date, origin, destination, mode, class, passenger count); the computed distance with method; the DEFRA factor reference and value; the resulting emission; and the row-level confidence. The same trail is appended to your published report.

When an auditor asks "where did this number come from", you can answer the question at the level of a single trip. That is the audit posture ClariComp was built for.
