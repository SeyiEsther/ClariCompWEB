01 · Distance calculation

Haversine for aviation. Distance Matrix for ground.

Flights are calculated using the Haversine Great Circle formula. This is the geodesic distance between two points on a sphere - the path an aircraft actually follows, give or take routing.

d = 2r · asin(√(sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁ · cos φ₂ · sin²(Δλ/2)))

where
  r   = earth radius (6,371 km)
  φ₁,φ₂ = latitudes of origin and destination
  Δφ  = φ₂ - φ₁
  Δλ  = λ₂ - λ₁

Road, rail and taxi journeys use the Google Maps Distance Matrix API to return real-route distance, accounting for actual road or track geometry. Where origin/destination is ambiguous, ClariComp falls back to the centroid of the named locality and flags the row.

02 · Emission factors

DEFRA 2025 GHG Conversion Factor tables, Table 5.

All factors are sourced from the UK Government's 2025 release of the GHG conversion-factor tables, specifically Table 5 - Business Travel. Each row in your ClariComp file is annotated with the factor reference used, so an auditor can trace any calculation back to a published source.

Factor updates are versioned. The 2025 release applies to reporting periods ending in 2025. Earlier periods use the corresponding earlier release - ClariComp does not retroactively apply newer factors to older data.

03 · Factor precision

Six decimal places. Carried through every step.

Rounding errors accumulate. ClariComp stores every factor at 6 dp, carries that precision through distance × factor, and only rounds at the final aggregate. The published report shows three decimal places of tonnes CO₂e; the audit appendix shows six.

04 · Confidence scoring

A score per row, aggregated per report.

Each row carries a confidence score from 0 to 100. The score is reduced when (a) the transport class is inferred rather than specified, (b) origin or destination is resolved to a centroid, (c) the trip is round-trip but only logged one-way, or (d) distance is not provided and Distance Matrix returns multiple plausible routes.

The aggregate confidence is the row-emission-weighted mean. A 98.7% confidence on a 47 tCO₂e total means the methodology applied is unambiguous for 98.7% of the emissions accounted for.

05 · Assumptions

What ClariComp assumes when your data is incomplete.

  1. Aircraft class defaults to economy if not specified. Business and first carry materially different factors; ClariComp flags rows where class is inferred.
  2. Trips logged with a single date but a return implied (e.g. day-trip Manchester ↔ London) are treated as one-way unless a return row is present.
  3. Car journeys default to "average passenger car, unknown fuel" if engine size and fuel type are absent.
  4. Rail journeys outside the UK national rail network use the European rail factor if the route is fully European, the long-haul rail factor otherwise.
  5. Where two airports serve the same city (London, Paris, Milan), ClariComp uses the airport name if provided, else the city centroid.
06 · Limitations

What ClariComp does not do.

ClariComp covers Scope 3 Category 6 - Business Travel - only. It does not calculate Scope 1 (direct fuel), Scope 2 (electricity), or other Scope 3 categories (purchased goods, transport of sold products, employee commuting, etc.). The preview output is indicative, not an audited statement.

If you need full-scope reporting or third-party assurance, ClariComp is not the right tool. The product is deliberately narrow - done properly, at one price - and we'd rather tell you that here than at the end of a sales call.

07 · Standards alignment

GHG Protocol Corporate Standard · Scope 3 Category 6.

ClariComp follows the GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting & Reporting Standard, with calculations specifically aligned to the Scope 3 Standard's Category 6 (Business Travel) - the category covering employee travel between sites and to client engagements in third-party transport.

08 · What this means for your audit

The formula trail, in practice.

Every row in your ClariComp file carries: the input fields (date, origin, destination, mode, class, passenger count); the computed distance with method; the DEFRA factor reference and value; the resulting emission; and the row-level confidence. The same trail is appended to your published report.

When an auditor asks "where did this number come from", you can answer the question at the level of a single trip. That is the audit posture ClariComp was built for.

09 · Transport mode reference

The physics behind the numbers.

Each transport mode operates under distinct physical constraints. Understanding these is what makes the DEFRA factor choices defensible — and what an auditor is implicitly checking when they ask how a number was derived.

01

Road Transport

Road vehicles handle the highest volume of global passenger transit. They operate using internal combustion engines, electric drivetrains, or hybrid combinations.

Vehicles: Passenger cars, buses, HGVs, motorcycles
Fuels: Petrol, diesel, electricity, LPG, CNG, biofuels
Typical emission figures
Average petrol car~7.5 L/100 km · ~170 gCO₂/km
Battery EV (UK grid)~15 kWh/100 km · ~53 gCO₂/km
Transit bus (per passenger)~82 gCO₂e/pkm
Core calculation
C = d × fc × EFfuel

where
  d          = distance travelled (km)
  fc         = fuel consumption rate (L/100 km or kWh/km)
  EFfuel     = emission factor per unit fuel (kgCO₂e/L or kgCO₂e/kWh)
02

Rail Transport

Rail is the most energy-efficient mechanical land transport mode due to low rolling resistance between steel wheels and steel rails.

Vehicles: High-speed trains, diesel-electric, freight, subways, trams
Fuels: Overhead/third-rail electricity, diesel
Typical emission figures
Eurostar / TGV~4.4 gCO₂e/pkm
UK national rail~35 gCO₂e/pkm
Energy intensity (electric)<50 Wh/pkm
Core calculation
E = P × t × η

where
  P   = traction motor power output (kW)
  t   = time at that power level (h)
  η   = drivetrain efficiency (0.85–0.92 for electric rail)
03

Air Transport

Aviation relies on high-energy-density liquid fuels to overcome gravity and aerodynamic drag at high speed. Takeoff and climb account for a disproportionate share of short-haul fuel burn.

Vehicles: Narrowbody/widebody jets, turboprops, cargo planes
Fuels: Jet A / Jet A-1 (kerosene), SAF, Avgas
Typical emission figures
Short-haul economy~151 gCO₂e/pkm
Long-haul economy~148 gCO₂e/pkm
Long-haul business~429 gCO₂e/pkm
Core calculation
dgc = 2r · arcsin(√(sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁·cos φ₂·sin²(Δλ/2)))

FC = (TSFC / (L/D)) × Wi × ln(Wi / Wf)

where
  φ,λ     = lat/lng of origin and destination
  TSFC    = thrust-specific fuel consumption of the engines
  L/D     = lift-to-drag ratio of the airframe
  Wi/Wf  = initial/final gross weight (fuel burns during flight)
04

Water Transport

Maritime shipping moves approximately 80% of global trade by volume. Massive payload capacity offsets low operational speed — but fuel consumption scales cubically with velocity.

Vehicles: Container ships, bulk carriers, tankers, cruise ships, ferries
Fuels: HFO, MGO, LNG, methanol, ammonia
Characteristics
Ferry (foot passenger)~19 gCO₂e/pkm
Fuel-speed relationshipFC ∝ v³ (cubic)
Bulk freight efficiencyLowest per tonne-km of any mode
Core calculation
RT = Cf × ½ρw × Aw × v²

FC = RT × v × SFOC / ηprop

where
  Cf    = hull resistance coefficient
  ρw    = density of water (1,025 kg/m³ seawater)
  Aw    = wetted surface area of the hull (m²)
  v     = vessel speed (m/s) — note FC ∝ v³
  SFOC  = specific fuel oil consumption of the two-stroke engine
05

Pipeline Transport

Pipelines are stationary, continuous systems for fluids and gases over long distances. Energy consumption depends entirely on fluid viscosity, pipe diameter, and topography.

Infrastructure: Oil pipelines, gas networks, water mains, hydrogen test lines
Power: Electric pump stations, gas turbine compressors
Key variables
Governing equationDarcy-Weisbach
Pressure loss driverPipe friction, diameter, length
Flow rate unitsm³/s
Core calculation
ΔP/L = f × (ρv²) / (2D)

Ppump = Q × ΔP / ηpump

where
  f       = Darcy friction factor of the pipe wall
  L, D    = length and internal diameter of the pipe segment (m)
  ρ, v    = fluid density (kg/m³) and flow velocity (m/s)
  Q       = volumetric flow rate (m³/s)
  ηpump  = pump or compressor efficiency
06

Human-Powered and Active Transport

Active transport relies on human metabolic energy from food intake. The bicycle is the planet's most energy-efficient travel mode. Above 20 km/h, aerodynamic drag becomes dominant for cycling.

Modes: Walking, running, road cycling, cargo biking, kick-scooters
Fuel: Human metabolism (glucose, glycogen, fats)
Typical energy figures
Bicycle~16 Wh/km (most efficient land mode)
Walking~80 Wh/km
Carbon footprint~21 gCO₂/km (food production lifecycle)
Core calculation
Emet = BMR × PAL × t

Pcycle = Cr·m·g·v + ½ρair·Cd·A·v³

where
  BMR    = basal metabolic rate of the individual (W)
  PAL    = physical activity level multiplier
  Cr     = rolling resistance coefficient
  Cd·A  = aerodynamic drag area (dominant above ~20 km/h)
  ρair  = air density (1.225 kg/m³ at sea level)
