How to use last year's spreadsheet data for carbon reporting

Most UK manufacturers already have the data they need to file a credible SECR submission. It just lives in five different exports, none of them designed for emissions accounting. This is the cleanest path from year-end spreadsheet to filed report.

Why last-year data is correct for SECR

SECR (Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting) requires you to report on a complete financial year. Half a year of data, or a year-to-date snapshot, will inflate per-row averages and miss seasonal patterns - the auditor will, correctly, refuse to sign it.

This is why ClariComp accepts last-year data only. If your reporting year is FY 2024-25 (ending March 2025) you upload data covering 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025 - a complete period. The preview reflects an annual total, not a projection.

What columns your spreadsheet needs

Five required, four optional. Any column order. Headers are matched automatically; ClariComp normalises them on import.

Required

  • trip_date - ISO date or any unambiguous UK-format date.
  • user_name - employee name or ID; used for hotspots only, never published.
  • from_address - city, airport, station, or full address.
  • to_address - same.
  • transport_mode - air, rail, car, taxi, coach, etc.

Optional (but recommended)

  • distance_km - if you already have it from your travel system.
  • class - economy, business, first; or vehicle size for cars.
  • fuel_type - petrol, diesel, EV, plug-in hybrid.
  • passenger_count - defaults to 1 for car journeys if missing.

Common errors and how to fix them

1. Round trips logged as one row

An expense system that records "London → Frankfurt and back" as a single row of cost will produce a one-way distance unless ClariComp can detect the pattern. Fix: split into two rows, each with the appropriate one-way leg. If you can't reliably split historic data, ClariComp will treat ambiguous entries as round-trip and flag them with reduced confidence - the flag is documented in the formula trail.

2. Aircraft class missing

Business class produces materially more emissions than economy. If the spreadsheet has no class column, ClariComp infers economy and flags the row. For a defensible filing, populate class from your travel-management system if you have one, or from cabin-class indicated on expense receipts.

3. Airport ambiguity

"London → Paris" is ambiguous: there are three Paris airports and four London ones. Distance varies, but factor doesn't. ClariComp uses city centroids when the airport is unspecified - fine for short-haul, less fine for hubs. If your finance team can populate the airport name, do.

4. Mixing financial year with calendar year

The auditor will reject a file that includes both FY 2024-25 trips and FY 2023-24 trips. Filter your export to the reporting period before uploading.

How to upload to ClariComp

Drop the file on the preview page. Header matching, validation and the preview happen in the browser within a minute or two. If validation flags any of the issues above, you'll see them clearly - with the affected row indices - before any emission is computed.
